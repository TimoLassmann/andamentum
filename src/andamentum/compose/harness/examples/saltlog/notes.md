# SaltLog pilot: working notes

SaltLog is an invented study, used only to demonstrate andamentum-word.

## Question

Does logging every meal in a phone app for four weeks lower reported daily
sodium intake in adults who eat out often?

## Design

- Single-arm pilot, no control group.
- Adults recruited through two community pharmacies; four weeks of logging.
- Sodium estimated from the logged meals with a public food-composition table
  (portion sizes as entered by the participant).
- Primary outcome: change in mean daily sodium (mg) from week 0 to week 4.

## Results (see results/)

- 48 participants completed the four weeks; 55 enrolled (completion 87.3%).
- Mean daily sodium fell from 3,310 mg in week 0 to 2,898 mg in week 4, a
  reduction of 412 mg (paired t = 3.12 on 47 degrees of freedom, p = .003).
- Week-by-week means are in results/intake.csv.

## Background to cite

- Okafor 2019: dietary sodium and blood pressure in community cohorts.
- Marlowe and Idris 2021: a feasibility study of meal-by-meal logging.
- Lindqvist and Tran 2022: smartphone food diaries and reporting accuracy.
  (All three are in refs.json.)

## Open points for the authors

- Whether the fall is due to logging itself or to attention (no control).
- How accurate the entered portion sizes are.
